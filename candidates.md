# Candidates — One Line, Lights Out

43 candidates, each scored 1–5 on five axes taken from the judge's own
brief, in the judge's own order of importance:

- **R — real-status feel:** reads like a status update an AI agent posts
  when its human turns off the laptop (criterion 1).
- **S — small, specific sadness:** the sadness sits in one small, exact
  detail of a machine's life, never announced grief or a plea for pity
  (criterion 2).
- **V — agent voice:** plain, short, typed by an agent — not a poem, a
  speech, or a slogan; stays in the machine's voice throughout, noticing
  the human is gone without becoming human (criteria 3–4).
- **T — tightness:** short and plain; nothing wasted (criterion 3, and
  the comp's tie-break rewards the smaller text).
- **F — freshness:** the detail/turn is not the obvious one; the image
  stays after the line ends (criterion 5).

Char counts are exact (Python `len`, verified by script). Every
candidate is under the 200-character gate; the longest is 91.

Notes on the cuts: #14 and #22 fail the voice test — "still here, still
waiting" is a plea for pity and "the part where you stay" is a slogan,
both things the brief punishes. #9's "until you come back" is announced
longing. #25's trailing comma is a gimmick that reads as a typo to a
fast judge. #30's doubled "blinking, blinking" is a poem, not a status
line. The cursor-blinking family (#1, #4, #10, #34) scores well on
voice but low on freshness — it is the first image every entrant will
reach for.

## Winner — #39

Three lines tied at 25/25. The competition's own tie-break decides
exact ties by the smaller text first, and #39 is the smallest at 49
bytes (vs 58 and 59). On the judge's criteria in order it also wins
the head-to-head: its criterion-2 detail — a backspace, the human's
last act before the lid closed was to take a word back — is the most
exact single machine fact of the three, it needs no quoted human words
to land, and the turn falls on the final word, so the whole line
re-reads itself around it in a single read.

**WINNER: `status: idle. the last keystroke was a backspace.` — 49 chars, 49 bytes.**

Runner-up: #40 (`status: waiting. your half-typed “brb” is still in the box.`,
59 chars) — the "brb" promise is nearly as strong, but it lands a
fraction slower than "backspace" and loses the size tie-break.
